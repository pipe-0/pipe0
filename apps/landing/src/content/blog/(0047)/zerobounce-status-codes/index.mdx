## What a validation actually checks

- Four checks in order, one line each: syntax (shaped like an email),
  domain (DNS has MX records), mailbox (SMTP conversation asks the
  server if the box exists), reputation (known trap, complainer, or
  disposable). Each status is the pipeline reporting where it stopped;
  teach that once and the table stops being arbitrary.
- One structural fact worth stating early because it confuses
  everyone: `accept_all` appears as a sub-status of `valid`, while
  `catch-all` is its own top-level status. ZeroBounce resolves some
  catch-all domains to confident answers and leaves the rest in the
  ambiguous bucket; their docs never state the dividing rule. Knowing
  both exist saves readers an afternoon of confusion.

## The reference table

- Full table from the current v2 docs, exact identifiers, one verdict
  column (send / suppress / depends, with the condition):
- `valid` (expected bounce under 2%): send. Sub-statuses worth notes:
  `gold` (high-engagement recipients), `alternate` (likely secondary
  inbox), `alias_address` (forwarder, delivers but person-matching is
  weaker), `role_based_accept_all`, `ai_agent_mailbox` (a 2026-era
  addition: the inbox is managed by an AI agent, deliverable but
  possibly never read by a human; worth a sentence, no other article
  covers it).
- `invalid`: suppress, never retry. Sub-statuses: `mailbox_not_found`,
  `failed_syntax_check`, `possible_typo` (check `did_you_mean` before
  discarding), `no_dns_entries`, `mailbox_quota_exceeded`,
  `does_not_accept_mail`, `unroutable_ip_address`.
- `spamtrap`: suppress, and audit how it entered your list (section
  below). `abuse`: known complainer; suppress for outbound even
  though it would deliver, because complaints outrank bounces in
  provider scoring.
- `do_not_mail`: depends by sub-status. `disposable` and `toxic`:
  suppress always. `role_based` (info@, support@): suppress for cold
  outreach, acceptable for transactional. `global_suppression`,
  `possible_trap`, `mx_forward`: suppress for outbound.
- `catch-all` and `unknown`: the hard cases, own sections below.
- Billing nuance, stated in the table's intro because no ranking page
  states it: only `unknown` results and duplicates are free (credited
  back). A `catch-all`, `spamtrap`, or `do_not_mail` result consumes a
  credit; it is a definitive classification, just not a sendable one.

## catch-all: use their own bounce table

- The mechanism in two sentences: the server accepts mail for any
  address, so mailbox existence cannot be confirmed by SMTP; common at
  security-conscious companies, which are often exactly the accounts
  you want. Scale: ZeroBounce reports more than 9% of the 11B+
  addresses it processed in 2025 came back catch-all, and rates above
  30% are normal in enterprise B2B lists.
- The most useful artifact in ZeroBounce's docs, reproduced: their AI
  quality score (0-10) maps to average catch-all bounce rates:
  QS 0 = 40% expected bounce, QS 4 = 20%, QS 5 = 7%, QS 8 = 3.5%,
  QS 10 = 1.3%. This is the segmentation policy hiding in plain
  sight: treat catch-all + QS >= 8 as near-valid, QS 5-7 as
  secondary-domain-only, QS 4 and below as suppress. Nobody in the ranking
  results connects this table to a policy; doing so is the post.
- Honest limits, from an independent test worth citing: Allegrow ran
  fictional names (hermione.granger@) against real employees at the
  same catch-all domains and both returned `valid`/`accept_all`, and
  measured a 33.1% false-positive rate on ZeroBounce's Verify+
  resolution of fictional addresses. No validator solves catch-all;
  the score manages risk, it does not remove it.

## unknown: free, and usually worth one retry

- What it means: the server did not answer cleanly (`greylisted`,
  `timeout_exceeded`, `antispam_system`, `mail_server_temporary_error`
  and friends). You were not charged; unknowns are credited back.
- The right move: revalidate after 24-72 hours. No first-party
  percentage exists for how many resolve on retry [ZeroBounce does
  not publish one; a self-interested vendor study claims roughly a
  third of risky/catch-all segments reclassify on re-verification;
  label it as directional]. Greylisting in particular is designed to
  clear on the second attempt.

## Role-based addresses, quantified

- The 2026 BounceZero study numbers (vendor-run, label as such) make
  the policy concrete: role-based addresses bounced at 52.1% vs 11.5%
  for personal addresses, with a 17x spread inside the category:
  contact@ 3.3% valid, info@ 13.4%, admin@ 40.6%, hr@ 57.1%. If you
  send to role addresses at all, send to contact@ tiers, never hr@.

## Spam traps: what a validator can and cannot promise

- Spamhaus's own taxonomy, compressed: pristine traps (addresses that
  never belonged to a person, seeded to catch scrapers), recycled
  traps (real addresses that hard-bounced 12+ months, then silently
  reactivated as traps), typo-domain traps, live traps. Their stated
  position: "spamtraps are never revealed by their owners", so no
  validator can promise complete detection; a trap hit is evidence of
  a list-hygiene problem, not bad luck.
- Consequences, sourced: pristine hits can trigger immediate
  Spamhaus SBL listing; recycled hits erode reputation gradually.
  The one preventable class is typo traps, which real-time syntax
  and `possible_typo` checks catch.

## Two policy recipes

- Cold outbound: send `valid` only; `catch-all` only with QS >= 8 and
  only from an isolated secondary domain; everything else suppressed.
  The ceiling you are managing: Gmail's 2024 bulk-sender rules demand
  a spam rate below 0.10% and treat 0.30% as the hard line; Yahoo's
  line is 0.3%. One bad catch-all batch can spend that entire budget.
- Transactional and product email: `valid` plus `role_based` plus
  `catch-all` are fine; the user gave you the address, and the
  deliverability risk profile is theirs. Suppress only `invalid`,
  `disposable`, and traps.
- One sentence on why they differ: outbound is judged by receiving
  servers on your worst sends; product email is judged on user
  intent.

## If you use a different validator

- The mapping table, because teams switch tools and the vocabulary
  shifts: ZeroBounce `status`/`sub_status` = NeverBounce
  `result`/`flags` = MillionVerifier `result`/`subresult` = Emailable
  `state`/`reason`. Good = valid / valid / ok / deliverable;
  accept-all = catch-all / catchall / catch_all / accept_all;
  indeterminate = unknown everywhere. Refund-for-unknown is industry
  standard (Emailable states it verbatim), so never accept a
  validator that bills unknowns.
