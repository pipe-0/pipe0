## Why agencies hit this wall first

- The multiplication argument: an in-house team runs one workspace
  and one bill; an agency runs N of everything, and platform pricing
  rarely pools across clients. Cost grows linearly with wins while
  retainers do not.
- The iteration tax, now structural: Clay meters Actions on every
  workflow step, AI prompt, and HTTP call, with no sandbox mode, so
  building and debugging a client workflow costs the same currency as
  running it. In-house teams amortize that across a year; agencies
  rebuild per client. Secondhand practitioner reports describe
  $800-1,000 burned in a single learning week [label as
  review-aggregated; primary G2 text was not directly verifiable].
- The segment's weight, from the one number available: 96% of
  agency-model GTM practitioners use Clay. The community that made
  the product big is the one most exposed to its pricing.

## What actually changed in March 2026

- The facts, from Clay's own published memo and pricing page, no
  editorializing needed: on March 11, 2026 the $349 Explorer tier
  (the standard agency choice) was eliminated; self-serve collapsed
  to Launch ($185/mo, 15,000 Actions + 3,000 Data Credits, 50k-row
  tables) and Growth ($495/mo, 40,000 + 6,000, unlimited rows);
  existing customers could stay on legacy plans but the window to
  switch between them closed April 10; mid-cycle top-ups carry a 30%
  premium (down from 50%).
- Quote the memo directly, because a vendor publishing its own margin
  admissions is rare and the transparency deserves credit even when
  the outcome hurts: the change is "intentionally revenue and profit
  negative" short-term, correcting a "2022 mispricing decision", and
  the Pro tier "had been losing money... for years."
- The framing gap the community identified: Clay's "90% of users
  never hit limits" reassurance is about median users; agency
  operators are the tail consuming most of the Actions. Both
  statements are true; only one describes you.
- Fairness note that also answers the "cli access" searches: Clay's
  July 2026 API, CLI, and MCP launch is available on every plan
  including legacy, with no extra credit tax. The automation gap that
  drove some agency searches has narrowed; the pricing structure has
  not.

## What an agency stack actually requires

- The rubric the rest of the post scores against, five requirements:
  per-client isolation without per-client platform fees; portable
  workflow templates (build once, deploy N times); usage-based COGS
  you can invoice through; API/CLI access so delivery can be
  automated; data portability at offboarding (the client owns their
  list).
- One sentence on why this differs from in-house needs: agencies
  resell the output, so unit cost and repeatability outrank UI
  polish.

## The alternatives, scored

- Clay with discipline (stay, but operate differently): template
  libraries, credit budgets per client, BYO API keys where possible
  (connecting your own provider keys drops that step's Data Credit
  cost to zero, per Clay's FAQ), legacy plan if you still hold one.
  Honest verdict: viable for agencies whose clients pay for Clay
  seats directly; the iteration tax remains.
- FullEnrich for pure contact-data agencies: $29-55/mo, flat rate
  card ($0.055/verified email, $0.55/mobile at Pro), unlimited
  seats. No workflow layer; link the fullenrich-vs-clay post.
- DIY on n8n plus provider APIs: real for engineering-heavy shops;
  link the open-source post for the true cost accounting.
- [pipe0 →] pipe0 against the rubric, factually: usage-based billing
  that charges only the provider that returned a result (from
  $49/mo, 20 free credits); 3-10x more cost efficient than
  traditional tabular tools, with social pricing that stays cost
  efficient at volume; bring your own API keys and use the API on
  any plan; the whole engine is scriptable (REST, TypeScript SDK,
  MCP), which is the terminal-native delivery the queries describe;
  sheets to 2M rows with point-in-time recovery, so a client
  deliverable survives a bad run. Disclosure sentence as always.

## The margin model

- Build the worked example agencies will screenshot. Assumptions on
  screen: 8 clients, 5,000 net-new contacts per client per month,
  work email + mobile waterfall, realistic hit rates.
- Price it three ways with the verified numbers: Clay (Growth tier
  plus both currencies; note honestly that per-enrichment credit
  costs vary by routed provider, cite Clay's own worked waterfall
  example of $0.20-1.00 per lookup), FullEnrich (flat card), pipe0
  (usage-based, success-billed). Show effective per-client COGS and
  margin on a standard retainer under each.
- Include the line item vendors never model: iteration. Estimate
  build-and-debug runs as a percentage of production volume (20% is
  conservative) and show what that costs where testing burns live
  credits versus where re-running a finished sheet is free.

## Migrating without dropping a client

- Sequence: pilot the new stack on one internal or lowest-risk
  client; export tables to CSV; map each workflow column to its
  equivalent pipe; run both stacks in parallel for one cycle on the
  same list; compare coverage and cost per delivered record; then
  move the book.
- Name the true switching cost so the advice stays credible:
  rebuilt templates, retrained operators, two to four weeks of
  parallel running. An agency that switches for price alone and
  skips the parallel run trades a pricing problem for a delivery
  problem.
- End on the operator's line: agencies do not need a cheaper
  workbench, they need enrichment priced like infrastructure,
  because on their P&L that is what it is.
