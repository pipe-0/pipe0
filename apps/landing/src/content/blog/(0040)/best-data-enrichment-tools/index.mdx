Data enrichment has become one of the core disciplines in sales and growth.
Many of today's systems depend on it:

- Prospecting new leads and finding their contact data
- CRM enrichment
- Signal engines
- Building sales automations

In data enrichment, we differentiate between two types of systems.

## B2B contact data

B2B contact data providers are simple databases returning email addresses
and phone numbers. Sometimes these systems work with a single database.
Other times they run the same lookup through several providers in sequence
until one of them hits. That motion is called _waterfall enrichment_.

### Best B2B contact data providers

#### Surfe – waterfall enrichments made in Europe

[Surfe](https://www.surfe.com) is a B2B data provider similar to FullEnrich.

Pros:

- Unlimited email validation on every plan, including the free one
- The GDPR-compliant lookup mode is a real answer for EU teams
- Good coverage for emails and phone numbers

Cons:

- Per-seat pricing

#### FullEnrich – waterfall enrichments

![FullEnrich pricing page starting at USD 55
per month](/media/blog/20260812-2.jpg)

[FullEnrich](https://fullenrich.com) is the specialist: a waterfall of 20+
vendors that finds emails and mobile numbers, and nothing else.
One credit buys a found work email, ten credits for a found mobile. Failed lookups cost nothing.

- Starts at $29/month for 500 credits (free trial with 50 credits)
- Work email: $0.055 per found email
- Mobile number: $0.55 per found mobile

Pros:

- Published, predictable per-unit prices; unlimited seats share one pool
- Credits roll over 3 months on monthly billing, 12 on annual
- Particularly strong on phones, with US and Canada ownership validation

Cons:

- Catch-all emails are billed as found, and unresolved catch-alls carry
  real bounce risk
- Emails and phones only; everything else needs a second tool
- Expensive for what it does

#### LeadMagic – affordable contact data

![LeadMagic pricing page in August 2026. Starts at USD 49.99 per month](/media/blog/20260812-4.jpg)

[LeadMagic](https://leadmagic.io) is an API-first enrichment layer with 18 REST
endpoints.

- Starting price: $49.99/month for 2,000 credits (100 free credits, no

Cons:

- No state-of-the-art user interface

## Data orchestrators

A more interesting category, however, is tools that not only allow you to
fetch contact data but let you perform enrichment, actions, and logic.

These tools allow you to search, enrich, and qualify in one motion.

_Here's an example_: You start by searching for a list of every CEO in New York City.
The list contains 10,000 prospects. Then you use enrichment to fetch their
LinkedIn profile. Next, you exclude CEOs who don't work for a tech
company.

The list shrinks to 5,000 users. Next, you fetch the most
recent LinkedIn posts of every user on this list. If they have posted about
AI, you qualify them. For every qualified CEO, you fetch their
phone number and add them to a sequence.

### Best data orchestrators

#### pipe0 – 50+ providers with one interface

![pipe0 pricing page start at $49 per month. The cheapest and most powerful orchestrator in this list.](/media/blog/20260812-1.jpg)

[Pipe0](https://pipe0.com) is a modern, AI-first data enrichment platform with great coverage and leading 
cost efficiency.

It is the only tool in this category with a full API and an MCP server. Its tables can hold
up to 2M records. Conveniently, tables in pipe0 can be point-in-time restored, a feature 
that is missing in comparable tools.

Data moves freely between the MCP server, the API, and sheets, so agents and humans work on
the same rows. Everything can be scheduled and turned into automations.

- Starting price: $49/month for 1,600 credits
- Work email: $0.02 per found email
- Mobile number: $0.08 per found mobile

Pros:

- 3-10x more cost-efficient than traditional tabular tools
- Bring your own API keys and use the API on any plan
- Sheets, API, and MCP are one engine, not three bolted-together products

Cons:

- Fewer templates and smaller community
- No native sequencer; it integrates with the common ones instead

#### Clay – Revenue system orchestration

![Clay pricing page in August 2026 starting at $167 per month](/media/blog/20260812-5.jpg)

[Clay](https://clay.com) has defined this category since 2017: a
spreadsheet where every column can call one of 150+ data providers, an AI
research agent in Claygent, and, since July 2026, a public API, CLI, and
MCP server on all plans. The March 2026 repricing split billing into two
currencies, actions for orchestration and data credits for purchased data,
and that split is the main thing to understand before you buy.

- Starting price: free plan; paid from $167/month billed annually ($185
  monthly)
- Work email: $0.25 per found email through a waterfall
- Mobile number: $0.75 per found mobile

Pros:

- The largest provider marketplace and largest community
- The free plan includes unlimited seats

Cons:

- Two currencies plus a 30% top-up premium make costs hard to forecast
- Actions expire monthly and don't roll over
- Extreme prices
- Practitioners measure the learning curve in weeks
- No working AI agent (Sculptor has not worked for us, ever)
- Many features hidden behind enterprise plans

#### Cargo – node-based enrichment

![Cargo pricing page in August 2026 starting at 165$ / mo](/media/blog/20260812-6.jpg)

[Cargo](https://www.getcargo.ai) sits closest to the data warehouse: a
node-based workflow builder with AI agents for research, qualification, and
SDR work, on top of 100+ integrations. You wire sources, enrichments, and
actions into graphs, and the agents run inside them.

- Starting price: From $165/month for 1,500 credits
- Work email: $0.16 per found email
- Mobile number: $0.65 per found mobile

Pros:

- No feature gating: agents, workflows, and SSO on every tier
- Top-ups and overage at plan rates, with no premium
- Orchestration steps are moderately priced, at 1 credit per 100 steps

Cons:

- Three billing meters (enrichment, orchestration, storage) to forecast
- Many workarounds because the core data model does not scale
- Around 15-20 data providers, compared to Clay's 150+
- Almost no public reviews to sanity-check quality against
- High prices
