Earlier today we published a test of
[ZeroBounce against MillionVerifier](/blog/zerobounce-vs-millionverifier) on
100 addresses where we know every answer. LeadMagic, which we mostly use for
finding emails and phone numbers, also sells validation, so we ran the same
100 addresses through it. It came out on top.

## The test, briefly

Fifty addresses are real: pipe0 users who signed in with Google in the 45
days before the test, 30 on company domains and 20 on Gmail. Fifty are fake,
generated for the test with no person behind them: random mailboxes at Gmail,
Outlook, and well-known company domains, and addresses at domains that don't
exist.

A verdict is right when it calls a real address valid or a fake one invalid.
Calling a fake valid is the costly error, because it bounces. Calling a real
address invalid loses a lead. Catch-all and unknown count as undetermined:
the verifier didn't commit, which is better than guessing wrong.

## The results

| | LeadMagic | ZeroBounce | MixRank | MillionVerifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Right answers | 97 of 100 | 96 of 100 | 93 of 100 | 66 of 100 |
| Real addresses confirmed | 49 of 50 | 47 of 50 | 47 of 50 | 31 of 50 |
| Fakes caught | 48 of 50 | 49 of 50 | 46 of 50 | 35 of 50 |
| Fakes passed as valid | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| Real addresses rejected | 1 | 2 | 3 | 1 |
| Undetermined | 2 | 2 | 4 | 32 |
| Median time per check | 2.0 s | 0.3 s | 1.3 s | 0.7 s |

LeadMagic and ZeroBounce are close enough that one address separates them,
and on 100 addresses that is within noise. What sets LeadMagic apart is where
its few misses fall.

## Every real address but one

LeadMagic confirmed 49 of the 50 real addresses. The one it rejected, every
verifier rejected, and MillionVerifier's reason was "no mailbox". A Google
sign-in 45 days ago proves the account existed then, not that the address
still works today, so that one is most likely our label, not LeadMagic's
mistake. Read that way, LeadMagic confirmed every real mailbox in the test.

For outbound, that is the number that matters most. A verifier that wrongly
rejects real work addresses quietly shrinks every list it touches, and you
never find out which leads you lost. ZeroBounce rejected two real addresses
and MixRank three.

## Unknown, and free

LeadMagic's two undetermined verdicts were both on fake addresses at
salesforce.com and atlassian.com, where it said unknown rather than guess.
LeadMagic has no separate catch-all status: an address on a domain that
accepts all mail comes back as unknown.

It also doesn't charge for that. A valid or invalid verdict costs a quarter
credit, between 0.18 and 0.5 cents depending on the plan, and an unknown is
free. ZeroBounce charges for catch-all results, and MillionVerifier credits
them back as a goodwill gesture its terms say it can withdraw.

## The trade-off is speed

LeadMagic took a median of 2 seconds per check, the slowest of the four.
ZeroBounce answered in 0.3 seconds. For a signup form, where a person waits
for the answer, that difference is noticeable. For cleaning a list before a
campaign or checking addresses in a batch, it isn't.

## What we'd use, and when

For cold outbound to work addresses, both LeadMagic and ZeroBounce are good
choices, and the test can't separate them on accuracy. LeadMagic is cheaper
per check and doesn't bill unknowns, ZeroBounce is much faster and comes with
compliance paperwork and deliverability tools. MillionVerifier stays the
cheapest option for consumer lists, where it was as accurate as the others,
but a third of its answers on work domains were catch-all.

In a pipe0 sheet you don't have to settle it once. Validation is a column, so
you can run it after the email waterfall on every new row and see the
verdict next to the address.

## What this test can't tell you

A hundred addresses is a small sample, and 30 real work addresses decide most
of the differences. Our fake company addresses sit on 20 large domains, and
your prospects' mail servers will behave differently. LeadMagic ran about
half an hour after the other three, so a mail server that changed its
behavior in between would affect the comparison. The full scorecards are in the reviews
of [LeadMagic](/reviews/leadmagic), [ZeroBounce](/reviews/zerobounce), and
[MillionVerifier](/reviews/millionverifier).
