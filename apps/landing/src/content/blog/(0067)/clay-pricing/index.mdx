The plan price is the floor, not the bill. Since March 11, 2026, every Clay
plan meters two separate things, and both can be sized up. What you pay comes
down to how many records you enrich and how much data each one needs. There
is also a Free plan, and Enterprise is priced on request.

## Clay's plans at a glance

| | Free | Launch | Growth | Enterprise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly billing | $0 | from $185 | from $495 | Custom |
| Annual billing, per month | $0 | from $167 | from $446 | Custom |
| Data Credits a month | 100 | 2,500 | 6,000 | 100,000+ |
| Actions a month | 500 | 15,000 | 40,000 | 200,000+ |
| Rows per table | 200 | 50,000 | 50,000 | 50,000, bulk above |
| Phone numbers, signals, schedules | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| CRM sync, HTTP API, webhooks | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| SSO, data warehouses | No | No | No | Yes |

Every plan has unlimited seats and tables, multi-provider waterfalls,
Claygent, and bring-your-own API keys. The old Starter, Explorer, and Pro
plans ($149, $349, and $800 a month) are closed to new customers; existing
customers can keep them.

## Two meters: Data Credits and Actions

Clay used to sell one credit for everything. Now it sells two.

_Data Credits_ buy data and AI from the providers in Clay's marketplace: an
email address, a phone number, a company profile, a Claygent run. They cost
about 4 to 5 cents each, and the price of an enrichment varies by provider and
data type, from half a credit to ten or more.

_Actions_ pay for the work. Every record enriched is one Action, whichever
provider answers. So is every AI run, every push to a CRM, every HTTP call,
and every record sent to a sequencer or an ad platform. An Action costs under
half a cent.

Some work costs nothing on either meter: importing your own data, CRM and
webhook imports, formulas, filters, and exporting a CSV. Searching for people
or companies to start a list uses no Actions.

Two rules shape the bill more than the prices do:

- Your own API key removes the Data Credit cost of an enrichment, but the
  Action is still charged.
- Actions can't be topped up and don't roll over. Run out, and the only way
  forward is a larger tier. Data Credits can be topped up and partly roll
  over.

## What a credit costs at each tier

Both meters come in fixed tiers. A larger tier is cheaper per unit, but not
by much: the largest Data Credit tier costs 15% less per credit than the
smallest.

| Data Credits a month | Monthly | Per credit | Annual, per month | Per credit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2,500 (Launch only) | $125 | 5.0¢ | $113 | 4.5¢ |
| 6,000 | $290 | 4.8¢ | $261 | 4.4¢ |
| 10,000 | $460 | 4.6¢ | $414 | 4.1¢ |
| 20,000 | $880 | 4.4¢ | $792 | 4.0¢ |
| 50,000 | $2,125 | 4.3¢ | $1,913 | 3.8¢ |

Actions are cheap enough that the tier rarely matters for the total. Launch
starts at 15,000 Actions for $60 ($4 per thousand) and goes up to 200,000 for
$540. Growth starts at 40,000 for $205, and at every tier its Actions cost 37%
to 57% more than Launch's.

Annual billing saves 10% on both. Top-ups, the way to buy more Data Credits
without changing tier, cost 30% more than your plan's rate.

## What one enriched record costs

Clay's own figure: a fully enriched record typically costs 6 to 20 Data
Credits. At 4 to 5 cents a credit, that is 30 cents to a dollar a record,
before Actions. A light enrichment is far cheaper. In Clay's example, a
LinkedIn profile costs half a credit and a work email another half, so a
profile plus an email lands near 5 cents.

At small volumes, the plan's minimum decides the bill. 1,000 records with a
profile and an email each need 1,000 Data Credits and maybe 5,000 Actions.
Launch covers that, so you pay $185, or 18.5 cents a record. The same records
fully enriched need 6,000 Data Credits, which moves Launch to the 6,000 tier
and the bill to $350. Add a HubSpot sync and you are on Growth at $495.

## The costs that surprise people

### CRM sync starts at $495

HubSpot and Salesforce integrations, HTTP API
calls, and webhooks are Growth features. A team that only needed Launch's
volume pays the Growth price the day it wants records in its CRM.

### Phone numbers are the expensive line

Clay calls them the costly data
type, and they are where a record climbs toward the top of the 6 to 20 range.
Free plans can't enrich phone numbers at all.

### Empty results are free only for finders

When an email or phone finder
comes back empty, Clay refunds the Data Credits and the Action. Many other
enrichments charge whether or not the provider finds a match, and a
verification result of "invalid" counts as an answer, so it is charged.

### AI depends on the model

Most models cost a flat number of Data Credits
per row: 0.4 for GPT-5 Mini, 1.5 for Claude 4.5 Sonnet, 7.5 for Claude 4.6
Opus. The heaviest reasoning models are billed on the tokens they use, so the
cost per row is known only after the run. Every AI run is also an Action.

### Tables stop at 50,000 rows

Larger lists mean splitting them across
tables, or Enterprise, which runs bulk enrichment above 50,000 records at
once.

### Rollover has a ceiling

Unused Data Credits on monthly Launch and Growth
plans bank up to twice the monthly amount; annual plans carry 15% into the
renewal. Unused Actions vanish at the end of every cycle.

## Five ways to pay less on Clay

1. Bring your own keys for the providers you already pay for. The Data
   Credits disappear; only the Action stays.
2. Size the Data Credit tier before you need top-ups. Top-up credits cost 30%
   more than your tier's rate, while the next tier costs less per credit.
3. Bill annually once your volume is steady: 10% off both meters, with all
   credits up front.
4. Gate expensive columns, like phone numbers, with run conditions, so they
   only run on rows that passed the cheap checks.
5. Pick finders that refund empty results, so a miss costs nothing.

## Is Clay worth the price?

Clay is the most complete visual workbench for GTM data, with the largest
template library and community in the category. If your team builds in the
browser, needs dozens of providers, and enriches a few thousand records a
month, the bill is predictable once you know your credits per record.

It gets expensive in three situations: a workflow that needs CRM sync at
Launch volumes, lists that need phone numbers on every row, and anything above
50,000 rows. It also assumes a person builds the table. Agents like Claude
Code can call Clay's API and MCP server, but they can't build tables through
them.

If cost is the problem, two directions are worth a look. For emails and phone
numbers only, a waterfall provider like FullEnrich charges per result found,
5.5 cents per email and 55 cents per mobile. For full Clay-style tables,
[pipe0](https://pipe0.com) runs the same kind of enrichment sheets, built by
you or by the agent you already use, at 6 to 12 times lower cost, with plans
from $49 a month.
