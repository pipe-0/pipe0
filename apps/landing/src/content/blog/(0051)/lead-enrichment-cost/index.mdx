## Nobody sells enrichment in the same unit

- Open with the unit problem, because it is why every "pricing
  guide" disappoints: vendors price per seat (ZoomInfo, LeadIQ),
  per credit (Clay, FullEnrich, Apollo), per record (PDL), or per
  success (waterfall APIs), and those units are not convertible
  without knowing your hit rate and volume. The post's promise:
  convert everything to cost per delivered field, the only unit
  a budget can be built on.
- The one-sentence enterprise finding: no enterprise vendor
  publishes per-record pricing at any volume, so enterprise
  numbers below come from contract-intelligence data (Vendr) and
  arithmetic, both shown.

## The four models, with real anchors

- Seat licenses: ZoomInfo median contract $33,500/yr (1,569
  tracked deals, range $7,200-$155,280, average negotiated
  discount ~22%; a different snapshot reports a $54k median,
  which is a lesson in dating your sources); Cognism $36,000;
  Lusha $15,999; LeadIQ $26,400 plus its de facto Sales
  Navigator dependency. Seats price people, not usage; a
  five-person team pays five times for the same dataset.
- Credits: the workbench and finder model. Anchors: FullEnrich
  publishes the cleanest card ($0.055 per found email, $0.55 per
  found mobile at Pro); Apollo and Hunter bundle credits into
  tiers with monthly expiry; Clay splits Data Credits from
  Actions and charges a 30% premium on mid-cycle top-ups.
- Per-record APIs: People Data Labs at $0.28 per person record
  (bills on match), $0.10 per company record; Hunter's
  pay-as-you-go slides from $0.05 to $0.0056 per credit by
  volume; Icypeas from $0.019 to $0.005 with credits that never
  expire.
- Per-success waterfalls: billing fires only when a provider
  delivers; misses are free. This is the model where quoted
  price equals effective price, which is the entire reason to
  care about the distinction the next section formalizes.

## Effective cost: the formula vendors hope you skip

- The formula, plainly: effective cost per delivered field =
  (money spent) / (records actually delivered), and the three
  things that drive it apart from list price are hit rate,
  expiry, and overages.
- Hit rate: single-source live find rates cluster 49-72% in
  published tests. Under attempt-billing at a 60% hit rate, a
  $0.05 lookup really costs $0.083 per delivered email. Under
  success-billing it costs $0.05. Same list price, 66% apart.
- Expiry: monthly-expiring credits are a use-it-or-lose-it
  discount to the vendor. UpLead's ToS says "use it or lose it"
  verbatim; Breeze Intelligence credits reset monthly; Hunter
  and Apollo expire monthly; Icypeas never expires. For seasonal
  pipelines, expiry can be a 20-40% effective surcharge; model
  your actual monthly variance against it.
- Overages and escalators: Clay top-ups +30%; ZoomInfo overage
  credits at $0.60 falling to $0.20 only past 2.5M; reported
  renewal uplifts of 10-20% on auto-renewing enterprise
  contracts with 60-day notice windows. Put renewal dates on
  the calendar the day you sign; that sentence is worth the
  post.

## So what does enterprise lead enrichment cost?

- Answer the query with the method plus a worked scenario.
  Method: take the Vendr median for the shortlist vendor, apply
  the ~22% negotiation norm, add modules you actually need, then
  divide by honest annual delivered-record volume to get the
  per-record truth. Most teams doing this for the first time
  find their database contract prices out between $0.50 and $2+
  per actually-used record; the silence around that number is
  why vendors do not publish it.
- The worked scenario, concrete: 50,000 contacts enriched per
  quarter with email plus mobile. Price it three ways with the
  anchors above: an enterprise database contract (median-based),
  a credit workbench (rate-card based, iteration included), and
  a success-billed waterfall. Show the arithmetic in a table;
  let the spread speak. [Numbers to be computed from this
  post's anchors at writing time; keep every input cited.]
- The recurring-cost coda with the decay math: B2B data decays
  ~2.1% per month (~22.5% a year), and about 30% of
  professionals change jobs annually, so enrichment is not a
  one-time purchase but a refresh flow. Annualize accordingly;
  a "cheap" one-time bulk buy that rots is the most expensive
  option on a two-year horizon. Link the Pipedrive post's
  refresh cadence guidance and high-volume-data-enrichment for
  the scale techniques.

## How teams actually cut this 3-10x

- The three levers, mechanism named for each: waterfalls (pay
  the best-fit provider per record instead of the union of
  subscriptions; coverage compounds while cost does not);
  success billing (align spend with delivered value; misses
  free); bring-your-own keys (keep negotiated provider rates
  while renting orchestration).
