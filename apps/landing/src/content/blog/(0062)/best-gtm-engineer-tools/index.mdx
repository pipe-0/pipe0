Every GTM engineering stack has the same building blocks: data and
enrichment, automation, sync, a CRM, and a sending tool. In
[a survey of 62 company stacks](https://intempt.com/blog/gtm-stack-2026),
Clay showed up in 71% of them, n8n in 48%, HubSpot in 39%, and Attio in 32%.

What changed is where the thinking happens. So for every layer below, the
question is the same: can your coding agent operate it?

## The stack at a glance

| Layer | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reasoning | Claude Code | Where your GTM logic lives now |
| Data and automation | pipe0 or Clay | Enrichment, lists, and automations |
| CRM | Attio, Twenty, or HubSpot | All open to agents |
| Sending | lemlist or Amplemarket | Multichannel outreach |
| Warehouse sync | pipe0, Hightouch, or Polytomic | Warehouse and tools in sync |
| Web research | Exa or Parallel | Web search built for agents |

## Reasoning: Claude Code

This is the layer that moved. Instead of encoding a play in Clay's table
columns, you describe it to a coding agent, and the plan lives in files you
can review, version, and rerun.

Claude Code is the default for answering research questions and building
small automations that run on your machine or in its cloud environment.

As things get bigger, you'll hit the limits of an agent-only approach:
iterating on and storing large lists, signals, webhooks, schedules.
Building those with Claude alone turns into real engineering work that slows
down the experiments you actually care about.

## Data and automation: pipe0

![pipe0: "Give your GTM team its AI moment"](/media/blog/brands/pipe0-ai-moment.svg)

[pipe0](https://pipe0.com) is the data and automation layer built for real
GTM workflows. At its core is a stateless, idempotent data API: ask for
data, get it back. Connect it to your agent and it gets contact data plus
access to the systems around it, like your sequencer, your CRM, and Gmail.

When you need signals, webhooks, emails, and schedules, move the same data
into a cloud-hosted, Clay-like table with up to 2 million rows and
point-in-time restore. Think of pipe0 as Terraform for automation, as an
API. Your agent reaches all of it over MCP, and on average it's 6–12x more
cost-efficient than Clay.

Clay is still the most common tool in this layer, and if your playbooks
already live there, it works. Its API is a remote control for the Clay
workspace, though, so it fits best when people, not agents, do the
building.

## CRM: Attio, Twenty, or HubSpot

Attio is built for agents from the ground up. Its SDK, API, and MCP server
make the CRM something your agent can read and write without a human in
between.

![Attio: "Welcome to agentic revenue."](/media/blog/brands/attio.svg)

[Twenty](https://twenty.com) is the open-source option: self-hostable, with
a built-in MCP server, for teams that want to shape the CRM themselves.
HubSpot is the default for smaller teams: free to start, with its own
[MCP server](/blog/best-mcp-servers-gtm). If your company already runs
Salesforce, keep it; what matters is that your agent can reach it.

## The rest, quickly

- Sending: [lemlist](https://www.lemlist.com) runs multichannel campaigns
  across email, LinkedIn, and calls. [Amplemarket](https://www.amplemarket.com)
  pairs outreach with its own B2B data in one AI platform.
- Warehouse sync: [Hightouch](https://hightouch.com) and
  [Polytomic](https://www.polytomic.com) keep your warehouse and your tools
  in sync, in both directions with Polytomic. pipe0 covers simpler syncs
  itself: read rows, enrich them, write them back.
- Web research: [Exa](https://exa.ai) and [Parallel](https://parallel.ai)
  are web search built for agents, both with MCP servers, for research the
  data providers don't cover.

For the MCP side of this stack, see
[the best MCP servers for GTM engineers](/blog/best-mcp-servers-gtm).

## How to pick

Give your coding agent one real job, end to end: "Find 50 accounts that
match our ICP, get the head of RevOps at each, and put them in the CRM."
Watch where it stops and asks for you. That layer is the one to replace.
