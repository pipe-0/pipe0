It's become normal to advertise waterfalls with 20 or more providers for
finding emails and phone numbers. More providers, more coverage. It sounds
right, and it's the clearest sign I know that a vendor has never run a
benchmark.

## Twenty providers, a few datasets

Most contact data providers don't collect their own data. They buy it from
the same handful of large sources and resell it under their own name, at
their own price. Chain twenty of them and you haven't asked twenty datasets.
You've asked the same few datasets twenty times whether they have a phone
number they already told you they don't have.

You can see this in our numbers. On 150 LinkedIn profiles, three cheap
providers (LeadMagic, Prospeo, and Aviato) found 62 mobile numbers between
them. Amplemarket or Crustdata had already found 61 of those 62.

## What adding providers actually does

We took the same 150 profiles and built the waterfall one provider at a time,
always adding the provider that found the most numbers nobody in the chain had
found yet.

![Bar chart: mobile numbers found as providers are added to a waterfall. Surfe alone 96%, plus Crustdata 96.7%, plus Aviato 97.3%, then Amplemarket, Forager, LeadMagic, Fiber, Wiza, and Prospeo each add nothing and coverage stays at 97.3%.](/media/blog/charts/waterfall-plateau.svg)

The best single provider found 96%. Two more providers added one number each.
The remaining six added nothing at all. That's a nine-provider waterfall where
six providers are decoration.

Work emails flatten out more slowly. On 120 LinkedIn profiles, the best three
providers reached 84%, the next three added about five points, and the last
three added nothing. More real gain than with phones, and still nowhere near
twenty. Two of those first three, Aviato and Mixrank, often disagree with
premium data, so some of that early coverage is probably wrong.

The list was pre-filtered for people who can be resolved, so every rate here
runs higher than on a cold list. The shape of the curve is the point.

## "But misses are free"

A fair reply I got on Reddit: a waterfall stops as soon as it finds a valid
result, and providers only bill on success, so the extra providers don't cost
anything.

On money, that's mostly right. On everything else it isn't.

Every provider in the chain is another request to wait for. In our phone
benchmark, median response times ranged from under a tenth of a second to
almost nineteen seconds per lookup. A row that misses the first ten providers
waits for all ten.

And the order decides accuracy. A waterfall stops at the first answer, so
every extra low-end provider is one more chance for a wrong answer to arrive
before the right one. I wrote about that in
[why the first provider decides accuracy](/blog/cheapest-first-waterfall).

## How to spot a benchmarked waterfall

It takes one question. Ask the vendor how much unique coverage each provider
adds on a real list, and why they're in that order.

A vendor that has benchmarked will show you something like the chart above.
A vendor that hasn't will tell you how many providers they have.

I've never seen a 20-provider waterfall with great coverage. Most are bad,
some are good. Yes, that includes the big ones. Our own waterfalls use a
handful of providers each, and a provider earns its place by finding data the
others don't. If you want to check that claim, run your own list through us
and through anyone else, and count.
