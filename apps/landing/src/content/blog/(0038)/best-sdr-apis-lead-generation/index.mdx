## What are the top SDR APIs for lead generation?

- Answer in the first sentence: it depends which of four jobs you
  are hiring for, and the best API for each job in 2026 is listed
  below with its verified price. The four families: company data,
  person/contact data, search and discovery, and verification;
  signals (job changes, funding, visitors) are the emerging fifth.
- One honest sentence about this content category before the list:
  seven of the eight articles ranking for this query are published
  by vendors who rank themselves first without disclosure. Every
  number here comes from the vendor's live pricing page or docs,
  fetched and dated, and where sources contradict each other the
  contradiction is shown.

## How these APIs charge (read before any pricing page)

- The four models, each with its trap. Per-request: you pay on
  misses too; fine at high hit rates, expensive for mobiles.
  Per-match (pay-on-success): the honest comparison unit is cost
  per delivered field; ask every vendor for it. Credits: prepaid
  abstraction over either; the two questions that matter are does
  a miss consume them and do they expire (Hunter and Apollo
  credits expire monthly; Icypeas credits never do; that
  difference is invisible on pricing pages and real at renewal).
  Contract-gated: no public price, sales call, annual minimum.
- The free-tier reality check no listicle prints: 50-100 free
  credits a month is one afternoon of SDR volume. Free tiers
  answer "does the API work", never "what will this cost".

## Which APIs offer pay-per-use pricing for prospect data?

- The direct answer, because the query deserves one: almost none,
  and the one famous strict example is dead. For contact and
  prospect data, the market is soft pay-per-use at best:
  self-serve monthly plans you can cancel (Prospeo, Icypeas,
  Findymail, LeadMagic, FullEnrich, Datagma), which is
  contract-free but not usage-only. Hunter is the honorable
  exception with a real pay-as-you-go calculator ($0.05 per
  search credit at 1,000, sliding to under a cent at a million).
- The cautionary tale, told plainly: Proxycurl, the category's
  true pay-per-use vendor, shut down in July 2025 after a
  LinkedIn lawsuit, and its pricing page was still live and
  selling credit packs a year later. If a blog sends you there,
  the blog is stale.
- Verification IS genuinely pay-per-use: ZeroBounce and
  MillionVerifier both sell non-expiring credit blocks with no
  subscription (and at 100k validations the spread is real:
  $649 vs $149).
- [pipe0 →] where pipe0 sits in this taxonomy, one paragraph
  with the standing disclosure: usage-based credits from $49/mo,
  billing only the provider that returned a result, 20 free
  credits without a card. Not a strict no-subscription PAYG, and
  this post just defined the difference, so say it: the $49
  floor buys the waterfall, the sheets, and the MCP server; the
  variable cost behaves like pay-per-success.

- Person/contact APIs: People Data Labs (free 100 records/mo; Pro
  $98/mo, $0.28/record; bills only on match) for embedding at
  scale; Apollo (Basic $49, Professional $99; API depth gated by
  plan; the famous free tier's fair use is 10,000 credits/mo with
  a verified domain) as the budget all-rounder, gotcha: credit
  fair-use math and master-key gating; Prospeo, Icypeas,
  Findymail, LeadMagic as the finder specialists (all
  success-billed; LeadMagic's 5-credit mobile ≈ $0.10 is the
  cheapest published mobile rate; link the Prospeo and Icypeas
  deep-dive guides).
- Company data: PDL company API ($0.10/record), Crustdata
  (real-time; credit costs are published but dollar prices are

  dry sentence), Coresignal (published tiers $49-$5,000/mo; link
  the coresignal post).
- Search and discovery, answering the "best llm search api"
  query directly: Exa is the LLM-native search API ($7 per 1,000
  searches; Websets, its entity-list product, prices ~$0.005 per

  matching a description" rather than filter queries. Gotcha:
  rate limits on the advanced endpoints are unpublished.
- Verification: ZeroBounce (from $0.0032-0.0195/validation by

  why this list leads with the self-serve APIs.

## The hit-rate section every listicle skips

- Single-source reality, from published tests rather than vendor
  claims: live-lookup find rates cluster between roughly 49% and
  72% (Findymail measured 49.2% on a live test; Hunter ~72%;
  Apollo 78% in one 1,000-lead test). Any vendor claiming 95%+
  coverage is quoting accuracy-of-delivered-records, a different
  metric; the conflation is the oldest trick in this category.
- The waterfall tradeoff, quantified by the best available test
  (14 tools, 5,000 contacts, ~36% catch-all): the waterfall
  product won raw coverage at 87.1% versus 71.2% for the best
  single source, but carried a 4.0% false-positive rate versus
  0.5-0.9% for the accurate specialists. Coverage compounds;
  so does risk, unless validation runs behind the cascade. That
  is the design argument for waterfall-plus-verification as one
  pipeline, and it is exactly how pipe0 chains find and
  validate pipes; one sentence, then on.
- Mobile numbers are structurally worse everywhere, with ranges
  worth printing: US tech buyers 55-75% match, EU 15-35%,
  LATAM/APAC often under 20%; in one same-sample test Apollo
  found 78% of emails but 41% of mobiles. Budget mobile
  coverage expectations accordingly, whatever the vendor page
  says.
- And the humility paragraph that builds trust: two vendor-run
  Apollo-vs-ZoomInfo benchmarks disagree even on which platform
  bounces less. Single benchmarks are noise; the only number
  that matters is the one you measure on your own 200-row
  sample, which the free tiers above exist to produce.

## The new client is an agent

- The stat that dates every older listicle: 13 of the 18 vendors
  checked now ship an official MCP server (Hunter's is free on
  all plans; Prospeo's and Icypeas's launched with zero
  third-party coverage; ZoomInfo's requires enterprise
  entitlements), yet only one of the eight ranking articles
  mentions MCP at all [verify Apollo's official MCP status
  manually; research passes conflicted].
- What changes when the client is an agent, three sentences:
  billing models matter more (an agent retrying across
  per-request APIs burns money silently; success-billed
  waterfalls cap the damage), response schemas matter more (an
  agent normalizing five vendors' shapes wastes tokens), and
  server-side cascades beat agent-side retries on both. Link
  the waterfall-MCP post for the full argument.
