You will hear people say that sales signals are useless. They aren't. Most
teams use them wrong.

A _sales signal_ is a unique point in time on a prospect's journey. It hints
at what a prospect is trying to achieve. The point of a signal is to determine
the prospect's demand for your product.

Before pipe0, I was Cursor's first Growth Engineer and built its signal-based
outbound engine. Here is what worked.

## There are two types of signals, and one beats the other

When people say "signal", they usually mean third-party signals: signals
derived from data that can be purchased or scraped.

The second type is the first-party signal. First-party signals come from
information that only your company has.

| Third-party signals | First-party signals |
| --- | --- |
| Job change | Product usage |
| News mention | Page views |
| Funding round | Downloads |
| Podcast appearance | Support requests |

If you have access to first-party data, use it for sales. It converts better.

## Stop pitching the research

The point of a signal is not to pitch the signal to the prospect.

What the prospect did is neither new nor interesting to them. They know they
changed jobs. They were there when the funding round closed.

Never write "I see you switched your job from Y to X", or "congrats on your
latest funding round". Use the signal to derive what's important for your
prospect now. Punch where it hurts.

Most signal-based outreach shouldn't mention the signal.

## Velocity wins

No matter your team size, if you're serious about signal-based selling, launch
3 to 10 signal-based campaigns per week and retire an equal number. Keep the
winners. Be ruthless.

Finding winners takes many iterations. A bad signal converts nothing. A
winning signal produces reply rates between 3% and 6%, more if people trust
your brand.

Many winning signals turn into losers over time. I never figured out why.
Don't cry about them. Keep marching forward.

## Compound signals

A signal exists to tell you what a prospect needs. Sometimes one signal is
enough. Sometimes it takes more.

Signals can be aggregated:

- Has the velocity of promotions increased at a prospect's company?
- Has there been an influx of departures?
- Did someone sign up for your app and invite a second person?
- Did someone hit a usage limit for the first time, then view the pricing
  page?

Each of these says more together than its parts do alone.

## Give the prospect space to breathe

Working with signals is about timing, not reactivity. Being the first email
that arrives after a funding round means nothing. That inbox is full.

Time your outreach for when you expect the prospect to be available and open.
Experiment with the gap between the signal and the outreach, the same way you
experiment with the message.
