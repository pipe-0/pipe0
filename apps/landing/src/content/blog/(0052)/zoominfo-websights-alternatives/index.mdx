## What WebSights is, and how it is actually sold

- The product in one line: ZoomInfo's IP-to-company identification
  script; visitors resolve against ZoomInfo's company graph and feed
  its workflows (audiences, intent, enrichment).
- The economics, verified: WebSights is not sold standalone. It
  enters at the Advanced plan, $24,995/yr for 1-3 seats ($2,500 per
  additional seat), and Elite at $39,995; the entry Professional
  tier (~$14,995) excludes it. Real median ZoomInfo contracts run
  $33,500 (Vendr, 1,569 purchases), and all-in costs of $30-60k are
  common once modules stack.
- The comparison that frames the post: dedicated visitor-ID tools
  start at $49-99/mo. If visitor identification is the primary
  need, the bundle is roughly a 20x overspend; if the database,
  intent, and org charts are the primary need, WebSights inside the
  contract is a reasonable add. Both readers exist; serve both with
  one honest rule.
- Context worth one sentence: ZoomInfo runs competitive SEO content
  against the standalone tools (its own "RB2B pricing" page), so
  much of what ranks on this topic is one vendor characterizing
  another; this post cites primary pricing pages throughout.

## The mechanism, and why nobody escapes its ceiling

- Reverse-IP in two sentences: the visitor's IP is matched against
  registries mapping IP blocks to organizations; solid for office
  networks, blind to residential and mobile IPs. The structural
  decline: typical company-level match rates run 30-60% today
  versus 80%+ a decade ago, with US office-IP coverage around 10%
  since remote work; an independent 1.2M-session study resolved
  46.8% of sessions but rated only a fifth high-confidence, with
  strong geography effects (US 58.8%, UK 34.1%, Germany 13.9%).
- The consequence, stated plainly: switching vendors buys workflow
  fit, geography strength, and price sanity, not an escape from
  the physics. A vendor promising 90% company identification is
  describing a different metric or a different decade. Link the
  RB2B post for the person-level mechanism and its US-only legal
  basis; this post stays company-level.

## The standalone alternatives, verified prices

- Dealfront (Leadfeeder): the EU-native incumbent; free tier (last
  100 companies, 7-day history), paid from ~€99/mo. Deepest
  workflow integrations of the standalone set.
- Leadinfo: from €69/mo; credit for honesty: its own docs say
  roughly 30% of visitors resolve to a company, the rare vendor
  that publishes the real number. Benelux/DACH strength.
- Snitcher: from $49/mo, bootstrapped since 2015, the value pick.
- Albacross: from €59/mo, Swedish, bundles outreach automation.
- The person-level adjacents, one line each with the caveat: RB2B
  (free tier, US person-level, Slack-first), Vector ($399/mo),
  Warmly ($10,000/yr minimum, no free tier despite listicles
  quoting $700-900/mo; their live pricing page says otherwise).
  Person-level is US-only for structural legal reasons; link the
  RB2B post rather than re-explaining.
- Present as a compact table: level, geography strength, entry
  price, free tier, notable angle. Every number from the vendor's
  live pricing page, dated.

## The decision rule

- Already on ZoomInfo Advanced or Elite for the database: keep
  WebSights, it is effectively marginal-cost inside the contract;
  spend the energy on activating the data instead.
- Considering ZoomInfo primarily FOR visitor ID: do not enter a
  $25k bundle for a $99 feature; buy standalone, and if the
  database need emerges later, negotiate with the Vendr medians in
  hand.
- EU traffic dominant: the EU-native company-level tools, which are
  also the compliance-comfortable choice.
- On the OperationsOS-adjacent searches, one honest line: data
  hygiene and routing needs point at a different category
  (enrichment and workflow tooling), not at visitor ID at all.

## An identified company is a signal, not a lead

- The activation gap, same as every signal product: "someone from
  Acme visited /pricing" becomes revenue only after you know Acme
  fits ICP, who to contact, and where to route it. Every tool
  above stops at identification; the pipeline after it is where
  switching costs pay back.
