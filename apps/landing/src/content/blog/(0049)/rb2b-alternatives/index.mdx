## How visitor identification actually works

- Two mechanisms, and vendors blur them, so separate them plainly.
  Company-level: reverse-IP lookup against IP-allocation and ASN
  registries. Person-level: a pixel correlates cookies and device
  signals against an identity graph (email-to-device mappings
  accumulated across the ad ecosystem), then enriches the match from
  professional data sources. RB2B's own support docs describe exactly
  this; link them.
- The structural decline nobody selling the category mentions:
  typical company-level match rates run 30-60% today versus 80%+ a
  decade ago, and US office-IP coverage is around 10%, because remote
  workers sit on residential IPs and mobile CGNAT pools thousands of
  people behind one address. A 1.2M-session study resolved 46.8% of
  sessions to a company, but only a fifth of those at high
  confidence; and geography dominates (US 58.8%, UK 34.1%, Germany
  13.9%).
- The takeaway sentence: expectations set against decade-old match
  rates are the number-one source of disappointment in this category,
  and the reason half the readers of this post are shopping again.

## RB2B's real numbers, with receipts

- Present RB2B's own three figures side by side with links, neutrally:
  the homepage claims 70-80% identification (a combined
  person-plus-company figure tied to the Demandbase partnership); a
  support article says 40-45% person-level on qualified US traffic
  plus 30-35% company-level; the plan-comparison doc lists contact
  coverage at 15-20% on Starter and Pro and 35-45% on Pro+. All three
  are first-party. The listicles quoting "5-20%" cite none of them.
- Verified pricing: Free $0 (150 resolutions/mo, company-level only,
  Slack only), Starter $79/mo (300 credits, LinkedIn URLs, no
  emails), Pro $149/mo (600 credits, business emails, HubSpot/
  Salesforce/Zapier), Pro+ $199/mo (premium resolution waterfall).
  Credits do not roll over. [The free tier's loss of contact-level
  resolution in January 2026 is single-sourced; verify before
  publishing.]
- Practitioner reality from Trustpilot, quoted: coverage ("not able
  to receive visitor data of more than 20 people" on 200-300 daily
  visitors) and billing ("inability to schedule cancellation",
  credits lost on cancel). Four reviews only; small sample, real
  pattern. Give RB2B its due in the same breath: bootstrapped to $9M
  ARR, 100,000+ sites, and it made person-level identification a
  category. This post is routing, not a takedown.

## Why there is no EU RB2B

- The legal mechanics in one tight section, because "GDPR-native" in
  listicles explains nothing: company-level identification rides
  legitimate interest because no personal profile is built.
  Person-level fails twice in the EU: the ePrivacy rules (Germany's
  TTDSG §25 is the sharpest version) require consent for the tracking
  technology itself, and GDPR requires a separate lawful basis for
  the resulting personal data; regulators have said legitimate
  interest cannot launder data obtained through tracking that itself
  required consent. Double consent, structurally.
- Consequence: every EU-legal tool is company-level (Dealfront,
  Leadinfo, Snitcher, Albacross), and every person-level tool is
  US-only by design, not by roadmap. RB2B's US-only stance is a
  compliance architecture, not a missing feature.

## Which wall did you hit?

- Coverage wall (want more matches): the other US person-level
  players, with verified prices and no invented match rates: Opensend
  ($400/mo, e-commerce/retargeting DNA), Customers.ai (priced on
  Klaviyo profile counts, e-commerce), Vector ($399/mo, "Ad Reveal"
  angle), Warmly ($10,000/yr minimum, no free tier, bundles chat and
  an AI SDR; note the listicles quoting $700-900/mo contradict
  Warmly's live pricing page). Honesty note the research could not
  settle: whether these vendors share upstream identity graphs is
  undisclosed by all of them, so treat "stack two for double
  coverage" as unproven; benchmark variance suggests panels differ
  at least somewhat.
- Compliance wall: the EU company-level tools, priced: Dealfront/
  Leadfeeder (free tier, last 100 companies; paid from ~€99/mo),
  Leadinfo (from €69/mo; their own docs say ~30% of visitors resolve
  to a company, a rare honest first-party number, credit them),
  Snitcher (from $49/mo, bootstrapped since 2015), Albacross (from
  €59/mo). ZoomInfo WebSights if you are already in that ecosystem;
  link the WebSights alternatives post for why standalone buyers
  should not enter it for this feature.
- Activation wall: not a vendor problem; next section.

## Identification is the cheapest step

- The reframe: "someone from Acme visited /pricing" is worth little
  until you know whether Acme fits ICP, who to contact, and where to
  route it. The visitor ping is a signal; the pipeline behind it is
  the value, and it is why teams churn off these tools (RB2B's own
  reported ~9% monthly SMB churn is the category's tell, not a knock
  on one vendor).
- [pipe0 →] the pipeline, concretely: take the identified company,
  enrich firmographics and score ICP fit, find the right person and
  a verified work email through a waterfall, check the CRM so known
  accounts route to their owner, push the rest to outbound. pipe0
  treats signals as first-class and runs that flow as an automated
  workflow from ping to routed record; link the signup-alerts
  example and building-a-user-enrichment-engine as adjacent recipes.
- End on the operator's test, one line: if your visitor-ID tool
  disappeared tomorrow and nothing downstream would notice, the tool
  was never the problem.
