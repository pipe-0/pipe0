[Clay](https://clay.com) is one of the most popular sales technology tools
today. Founded in 2017 by Kareem Amin, Nicolae Rusan, and Varun Anand, it
gained traction in 2021. Today, it has grown into a company valued at USD 7B.

It is used by companies such as Anthropic, OpenAI, and Cursor.

## The Clay product

Clay's core offering is tables that can grow to 50k rows, which its users use
to find and [enrich](/blog/what-is-data-enrichment) sales prospects.

A common workflow looks like this:

> Open clay.com, create a table, find every VP of Growth who works at a
> company using Salesforce.com, find their email address, and draft
> personalized outreach.

## Automations

Next to building lists, Clay provides various interfaces to create
automations. Connect your table to a webhook and trigger a workflow when an
event happens outside of Clay, e.g., you receive an email.

## Orchestration, not a home for your data

Clay integrates with your entire GTM technology stack: warehouse, CRM,
outreach tools. As such, it is great at orchestration. This includes finding
people and moving them into an outreach sequence, searching your CRM, or ingesting data from tools like HeyReach.

Most often, Clay is used as a pass-through layer. Grab some data, do
something with it, push it somewhere else.

Less frequently, it is used as the home for your data. In fact, reading data
from a Clay table, as you would from a database or CRM, is challenging.

## B2B data

One of the differences between Clay and providers like
[Zapier](https://zapier.com) and [n8n](https://n8n.io) is that Clay offers
managed connectors that allow you to access different data sources without
purchasing subscriptions.

Using Clay's managed connections, you can access vendors like
[LeadMagic](https://leadmagic.io) or [Findymail](https://findymail.com).

To avoid paying a premium on managed data vendors, users can connect
subscriptions they have purchased with these providers directly. When doing
so, they are charged an action fee instead of Clay's managed rate.

## Platform extensions

Since its inception, Clay has expanded horizontally. This includes several
new products: [intent signals](https://www.clay.com/signals),
[CRM enrichment](https://www.clay.com/use-cases/crm-enrichment), and
[workflows](https://www.clay.com/blog/the-story-of-clay-workflows).

## APIs and agents

In 2026, Clay launched its long-awaited [API](https://developers.clay.com/)
and MCP server. Unfortunately, Clay's programmatic story is its weakest
part. Only a fraction of the platform is exposed via the API and MCP server,
and agents struggle to configure production-grade workflows.

## Alternatives

Several companies compete with Clay's core offering. 

### Pipe0.com

Offers Clay-like tables that can scale up to 2M rows and a full story around 
programmatic access and use by agents.

### Apollo.io

Build around it's proprietary dataset that allows for most cost effective enrichment at scale.
