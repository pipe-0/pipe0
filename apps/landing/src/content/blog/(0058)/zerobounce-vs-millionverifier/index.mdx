Every email verifier says it is 99% accurate. ZeroBounce guarantees 99.6%,
MillionVerifier promises "99%+". Neither number tells you what happens on
your list, so we built a list where we already knew every answer and asked
them.

## The test

We took 100 addresses. Fifty were real: pipe0 users who had signed in with
Google in the previous 45 days, which proves the mailbox existed recently.
Thirty were on company domains and twenty on Gmail. The other fifty were
fake, generated for the test with no person behind them: twenty random
mailboxes at Gmail and Outlook, twenty at well-known company domains such as
stripe.com and atlassian.com, and ten at domains that don't exist.

Every address went to ZeroBounce, MillionVerifier, and, as a third reference,
MixRank's verification API. We scored each verdict against the known answer.
A "valid" on a real address or an "invalid" on a fake one is right. Passing a
fake as valid is the expensive error, because it bounces. Rejecting a real
address is the other one, because it is a lead you never contact. Catch-all
and unknown count as undetermined, not wrong: the verifier declined to
commit.

## The results

| | ZeroBounce | MillionVerifier | MixRank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Right answers | 96 of 100 | 66 of 100 | 93 of 100 |
| Real addresses confirmed | 47 of 50 | 31 of 50 | 47 of 50 |
| Fakes caught | 49 of 50 | 35 of 50 | 46 of 50 |
| Fakes passed as valid | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| Real addresses rejected | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| Undetermined | 2 | 32 | 4 |
| Median time per check | 0.3 s | 0.7 s | 1.3 s |

The headline gap, 96 against 66, hides where it comes from. Both verifiers
were perfect on the easy half of the list. All 20 real Gmail addresses came
back valid, and all 20 fake Gmail and Outlook mailboxes and all 10 dead
domains came back invalid, on both.

Every difference was on work domains.

## Where they split

Of the 30 real work addresses, ZeroBounce gave a definite answer on 29.
MillionVerifier confirmed 11 and called 18 catch-all. On the 20 fake work
addresses, the pattern repeated: ZeroBounce caught 19, MillionVerifier caught
5 and called 13 catch-all.

A catch-all verdict means the company's mail server accepted the test
without confirming the mailbox, so the verifier can't tell a real address
from a made-up one. Many company domains behave that way, and some verifiers
are better than others at seeing through it. In this test, ZeroBounce and
MixRank saw through it on almost every domain, and MillionVerifier on about
a third.

That matters for what you send. Following MillionVerifier strictly, sending
only to its valid results, you would have dropped 19 of the 50 real
addresses. Following it loosely, sending to catch-alls too, you would have
sent to 14 of the 50 fakes: 14 bounces in 63 sends. ZeroBounce's valid
results contained no fakes at all.

MillionVerifier's caution has an upside. It rejected only one real address,
the fewest of the three. It isn't making mistakes on work domains, it is
declining to answer.

## What it costs

MillionVerifier is much cheaper, and it has no subscription.

| Checks | ZeroBounce | MillionVerifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | $129 | $39 |
| 100,000 | $649 | $149 |
| 1,000,000 | $3,199 | $449 |

That is about three times cheaper at 10,000 checks and seven times at a
million. MillionVerifier's credits never expire, and it credits back unknown
and catch-all results. ZeroBounce charges for catch-alls, and for accounts
created after June 1, 2026, its credits expire two years after the last
purchase unless you subscribe.

## Which one to use

Use MillionVerifier for consumer lists: newsletters, signups, anything that
is mostly Gmail and Outlook. It was exactly as accurate as ZeroBounce there,
at a fraction of the price.

Use ZeroBounce for work addresses and cold outbound. On a list of company
emails, a third of MillionVerifier's answers will be catch-all, and you'll
need a second check for those anyway. Paying more per check for an answer
is cheaper than a bounced campaign.

If you do both, you don't have to pick. In a pipe0 sheet, both are pipes, so
you can run MillionVerifier on consumer rows and ZeroBounce on work rows of
the same list.

## What this test can't tell you

A hundred addresses is a small sample, and 30 real work addresses decide
most of the gap. One real address was rejected by all three verifiers. A
sign-in 45 days earlier proves the Google account existed then, not that the
address still works, so that one may be our labelling error rather than
theirs. Our fake company addresses sit on twenty large domains; your
prospects' mail servers will behave differently. Run your own list of known
answers before you commit, and see the
[ZeroBounce review](/reviews/zerobounce) and
[MillionVerifier review](/reviews/millionverifier) for pricing details and
the full scorecard.
