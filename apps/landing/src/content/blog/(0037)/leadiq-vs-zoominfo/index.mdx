Most articles frame LeadIQ as the cheap alternative to ZoomInfo. The
contract data says otherwise: buyers report a median of $26,400 a year
for LeadIQ and $33,500 for ZoomInfo. At enterprise scale these tools
cost about the same. What separates them is where your prospecting
starts.

## A capture tool and a database

LeadIQ is built around a Chrome extension. A rep opens a profile in
LinkedIn Sales Navigator, LeadIQ finds a verified email and phone
number, and one click pushes the contact to Salesforce, HubSpot,
Outreach, or Salesloft. Discovery happens in LinkedIn; LeadIQ handles
the capture.

That design has a cost nobody puts on the invoice: LeadIQ without
Sales Navigator is close to useless, and Sales Navigator runs about
$960 per rep per year. Add it to any LeadIQ quote you model.

ZoomInfo is a database. You search its dataset directly, plan
territories, build lists, and export. It has org charts, intent
signals, and direct dials that LeadIQ does not. It works without
LinkedIn entirely.

## Comparing on cost

A warning before the numbers: the blogs ranking for this comparison
quote LeadIQ prices between $15 and $130 a month. The live pricing
page says something else. LeadIQ's Pro plan starts at $200 a month for
200 credits, with a free tier of 50 credits a month.

The credit math is where teams get surprised. An email costs 1 credit.
A phone number costs 10. At the $200 floor, a credit costs a dollar,
so 200 credits buys 200 emails, or 20 phone numbers, in a month. A
phone-heavy SDR team burns the allocation in the first week and finds
out in the second. Scaling the plan up brings the credit price down to
about 7¢, which changes the math but not the ratio.

## Coverage, by their own numbers

Buried on LeadIQ's pricing page sits the most useful disclosure in
this market: an 85% global match rate, and 70% in EMEA. A vendor
publishing its own regional weak spot is rare, and it deserves credit.
It also settles the question for European teams: expect three misses
in ten.

ZoomInfo publishes no comparable number. Its strength is US
enterprise, where its direct-dial coverage has no real substitute; its
own docs concede accuracy drops for startups, healthcare, and non-US
markets. Reviewers report real-world bounce rates of 15-25% on
exported lists. Test on your own accounts before you sign anything.

## Should you use either?

At [pipe0](https://pipe0.com) we benchmark enrichment providers and
have built enrichment systems at companies like Cursor and Cloudflare.
Our reading here is simpler than most comparisons make it.

If your reps live in Sales Navigator, buy LeadIQ. The capture workflow
is genuinely good, reps learn it in a day, and the free tier answers
the coverage question on your own accounts. Watch the phone credits.

If you plan territories from a dataset and need org charts and intent,
buy ZoomInfo, and budget for the whole contract, not the entry tier.

Running both is common in enterprise teams and is not a failure. One
maps the accounts, the other captures the people.

Both, though, price by the seat. If your prospecting is becoming
automated, running through workflows and agents instead of reps
clicking profiles, the seat model stops making sense. That is the case
where we would point you to [pipe0](https://pipe0.com): the same
contact data, found in waterfalls, billed per result instead of per
person.
