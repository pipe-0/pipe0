If you are active within the context of B2B data, you have heard the term
_waterfall enrichment_. But what is it, and how has it become the industry
standard?

Working with B2B data is challenging. Lead databases are incomplete, serve
wrong data, or trap users with unexpected price hikes. For years now, it has
become increasingly easy to become a B2B data provider.

Scraping providers like Bright Data, Coresignal, or Crustdata offer their data
as resellable data bricks. New providers purchase one or more of these data
bricks, add their own brand and marketing on top, and call themselves a data
provider.

Consumers are left to derive strategies to turn unreliable systems into the
fuel that powers their GTM operations.

One of these strategies is to chain multiple providers together in the hope
that if the first provider won't find the data, the second will.

## Waterfall

Waterfalls make use of the way B2B data providers bill. Most providers will
only charge for an email address or phone number if it is found. No result. No
charge.

This is an open invitation to chain many providers together without
meaningfully increasing the cost. All we drive up is the round-trip time. The
more providers we ask, the longer we may need to wait.

Waterfalls come in different flavors, and different providers sell them at
different price points.

This blog post aims to serve as your map to navigate the market, so you can
make an informed buying decision.

## Waterfall schemas

### The perfect waterfall

Only a few providers scrape the data themselves. It is much more common to
purchase enrichment data from a vendor that purchased it from another vendor.

But that raises the question of redundancy.

If you chain vendors A and B, but both resell dataset C, your waterfall is
pointless. Both providers ask provider C.

A perfect waterfall chains _n_ providers together. In this chain, every
provider contributes a meaningful amount of exclusive data to your waterfall.

Building such a waterfall requires careful benchmarking. Something that almost
no provider in the wider enrichment industry does.

### The foul waterfall

If a perfect waterfall is a chain of _n_ providers where each provider adds
meaningful exclusive data to the waterfall, a foul waterfall is a waterfall
that chains an absurd number of providers with overlapping datasets together.
This waterfall will waste a lot of your time, but you'll hardly gain anything
by chaining these providers.

## Waterfall offerings

### The embedded waterfall

Over the last few years, several providers have specialized in providing
waterfalls. A few notable mentions are Surfe, FullEnrich, and BetterContact.

The providers will manage a waterfall for you and charge a flat fee for each
resolved value. Typically, this is anywhere from 30¢ to 100¢ per phone number
and 8¢ to 30¢ per work email.

Typically, these providers all follow the same playbook. They resolve contact
information from a LinkedIn URL. Before calling the first provider, they
resolve the LinkedIn profile of the user, retrieve their profession and
location, and, based on this data, adjust the final waterfall.

### The frontloaded embedded waterfall

This strategy is a cost optimization over the embedded waterfall. Here, we're
placing a fast and cheap provider before an embedded waterfall. This provider
is often part of the embedded waterfall itself. By placing it before an
embedded waterfall, we don't gain accuracy or coverage. Instead, we save
roughly 75% of the cost for the 60% of values that are easy to resolve. Only
for our hardest-to-resolve values do we call the most expensive embedded
waterfall.

## How to evaluate waterfalls

We've previously written an entire [blog post]() about how to evaluate data
enrichment providers.

The core challenge when doing so is to create and run benchmarks that tell you
whether:

- A provider contributes meaningful unique data to your waterfalls
- The data that a provider returns is accurate

## What waterfalls cannot do

A common misconception is that waterfalls increase accuracy. This is not true,
or at least it is a more complex assessment.

Generally, waterfalls are a way to increase coverage, not accuracy.
