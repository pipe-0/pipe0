## Rows or answers: know which you are buying

- The organizing split: raw-data vendors sell the dataset (millions
  of rows, files or firehose APIs, licensed); enrichment tools sell
  answers about records you name. Bulk is expensive upfront and
  cheap per row; per-record is cheap to start and priced per answer.
  Buying the wrong shape is the five-figure mistake this post
  prevents; the worked math below makes the crossover concrete.

## What Coresignal actually is

- The verified profile: 70M+ company records, 895M+ employee
  records, 468M+ job postings, collected since 2016, in three
  processing tiers (Base raw, Clean deduped, Multi-source merged);
  refresh daily to monthly by dataset; delivery as files (JSON,
  JSONL, CSV, Parquet), cloud-bucket push, or API; a founding
  member of the Ethical Web Data Collection Initiative.
- Two honest readings of its own numbers, offered as method: the
  "4.5B+ records" headline is roughly triple the sum of its entity
  counts, because it counts historical snapshots; and a competitor
  comparison claims records for smaller companies "can be 3-4
  months old at point of access" [third-party claim, label it].
  Neither is damning; both are why you evaluate on a sample keyed
  to YOUR universe, not the vendor's demo slice.
- The genuinely rare thing, credit it: Coresignal publishes its
  API price list, $49/mo (2,500 credits) to $5,000/mo (10M),
  with per-record credit costs (jobs 1, Base 10, Multi-source 20).
  Most of this category is quote-only; published prices are worth
  something by themselves.

## The axes that decide a bulk purchase

- The rubric: entity coverage and geography; refresh cadence AND
  freshness distribution (average age hides the tail); sourcing
  method and compliance posture; delivery format; and licensing,
  the one that bites. Two specific license questions to ask in
  writing: can we redistribute derived works, and what happens to
  the data at contract end. Coresignal's public terms cover only
  its website content; the actual data license is a signed
  agreement, which is normal, but it means nothing you read on the
  site answers those two questions.
- Why licensing deserves its own paragraph: the canonical cautionary
  tale is the 2019 exposure of 622M email addresses traced to a
  People Data Labs-sourced dataset sitting on someone's unsecured
  server. Not PDL's breach, and that is exactly the point: once
  rows leave the vendor, the license and your handling are the only
  controls left.

## The alternatives, with verified facts

- Crustdata: the freshness play; 1B+ profiles claimed in a
  live-searchable index, real-time positioning, an official MCP
  server, YC-backed and growing fast; no public dollar pricing
  (credit costs are published, the price of a credit is not). Best
  when recency beats bulk scale; link the crustdata-mcp post.
- People Data Labs: per-record APIs with published entry pricing
  ($98/mo, ~$0.28 per person record; company records ~$0.10),
  mature licensing motion, bulk feeds by contract. Best for
  embedding data in products at moderate volume.
- Bright Data: dataset marketplace with the clearest public bulk
  unit price found ($250 minimum for ~100k records, around
  $0.0025/record); you take on more of the operational and
  compliance burden. Best for one-off large slices.
- OpenCorporates and registry data: the compliance-first choice for
  legal-entity ground truth (from £2,250/yr); narrow fields, high
  defensibility.
- Note the pattern in this category's content while citing it: the
  ranking "Coresignal alternatives" pages are mostly competitors
  reviewing each other, and the most detailed one gets Coresignal's
  tier names, prices, and trial terms all wrong against the live
  page. Check every number on the vendor's own site, including
  ours.

## The worked economics

- The crossover math with verified prices, for 50,000 specific
  records: per-record via Coresignal's API, Multi-source tier,
  costs 1M credits, which is the $1,500/mo Premium plan, so
  $0.03/record ($0.015 on Base). PDL at published Pro rates would
  be ~$0.28/record, 10-20x more, though real 50k contracts
  negotiate down. A Bright Data bulk slice prices around $0.0025/
  record with a $250 floor, but you inherit refresh forever.
- The rule that falls out: below ~100k records of a defined
  universe, per-record APIs win on total cost of ownership once
  refresh labor is priced in; at millions of rows for model
  training or market-wide analytics, bulk wins and nothing else is
  close. Price both shapes for your actual query pattern; the
  arithmetic usually decides.

## The legal ground, briefly

- The two cases that define the category, factually: hiQ v.
  LinkedIn established that scraping public data is not a CFAA
  violation, and hiQ still lost the war on contract and tort
  claims, ending in a $500,000 judgment. LinkedIn sued Proxycurl
  in January 2025 over alleged fake-account collection; Proxycurl
  shut down that July at roughly $10M ARR. Buyer's takeaway: the
  risk lives in sourcing methods, so prefer vendors that document
  public-only collection and answer sourcing questions in writing.
  Coresignal's EWDCI membership and published exclusions (no data
  behind logins) are the right kind of signal; verify equivalents
  from any alternative.

## When you do not need bulk data at all

- The reframe made concrete: if the job is "answer questions about
  the 50k accounts in our CRM" rather than "model the whole
  market," licensing a 900M-row dataset buys 99.99% waste plus a
  refresh treadmill.
- [pipe0 →] scoped honestly: for the per-record case, pipe0 runs
  waterfall enrichment across 50+ curated providers (Crustdata
  among them), billed per successful result from $49/mo, with
  sheets, API, and MCP as the surfaces. For the bulk case (model
  training, market-wide analytics), buy bulk; that is what it is
  for, and pipe0 is not that tool. End on the purchasing
  heuristic, nothing more.
