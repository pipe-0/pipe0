## What is Emailable?

Emailable checks whether an email address will accept mail before you send
to it. It started in New York in 2017 as Blaze Verify, merged with
TheChecker and took the Emailable name in 2021, and says more than 300,000
businesses use it.

You can upload a list, call one endpoint per address, or add a widget to a
signup form. Each check returns one of four states (deliverable, risky,
undeliverable, or unknown) with a reason, such as `rejected_email` or
`low_deliverability`. The same credits also buy inbox placement reports and
blacklist monitoring.

## How it did in our test

We ran 100 labelled addresses through six verifiers. Fifty were real: pipe0
users who had signed in with Google in the previous 45 days, 30 on work
domains and 20 on Gmail. Fifty were fake: random mailboxes at Gmail, Outlook,
and well-known company domains, and addresses at domains that don't exist.

On the easy half, Emailable was perfect. All 20 real Gmail addresses came
back deliverable, and all 20 fake freemail addresses and all 10 dead
domains came back undeliverable, at a median of 0.3 seconds a check.

Work addresses were different. Of 30 real work addresses, Emailable
confirmed 16 and called 12 risky because the domain accepts all mail, which
we count as catch-all. Of 20 fake ones, it caught 4 and called 14
catch-all. Its verdicts matched MillionVerifier's on 91 of 100 addresses,
against 73 for ZeroBounce and Bouncer, which resolved most of the same
domains.

That leaves two bad choices on a work list: send only to its deliverable
results and lose 14 of 50 real leads, or send to the catch-alls as well
and let 15 fakes through. One of its deliverable verdicts was itself a fake,
a random mailbox at hubspot.com that Bouncer also passed.

Emailable has an optional accept-all check that it says slows each request.
We ran it on all 50 work addresses. It resolved none of the catch-all
verdicts and moved 6 real addresses from deliverable to catch-all, so we
scored the default.

## What it costs

Pay-as-you-go starts at $38 for 5,000 checks, the minimum purchase, and
falls to $60 for 10,000 and $2,100 for a million. A monthly subscription
takes 15% off, and unused credits roll over. New accounts get 250 free
credits.

At 10,000 checks it costs the same as [Bouncer](/reviews/bouncer), about
half of ZeroBounce ($129), and more than MillionVerifier ($39). Credits
never expire, and credits spent on unknown results and duplicates in a
list come back when the list finishes.

## Emailable for developers and agents

The real-time API allows 25 requests a second and takes a timeout of 2 to
10 seconds. When a check runs long, it answers with status 249 and asks you
to send the request again, which is free for five minutes. A batch API
takes up to 50,000 addresses. There are official libraries for Ruby, Node,
and Python, and a CLI.

There is no MCP server. Instead, the CLI installs an agent skill for Claude
Code, Codex, and OpenCode, so an agent that can run shell commands can
verify an address. Emailable says it is SOC 2 Type II certified, with
documents in its trust center.

## Who should buy Emailable

Buy Emailable if you clean newsletter, ecommerce, or signup lists that are
mostly consumer addresses, and you want a fast check with SOC 2 paperwork
behind it.

Look elsewhere if your list is work addresses for cold outbound. For the
same price, [Bouncer](/reviews/bouncer) resolved far more of them;
[ZeroBounce](/reviews/zerobounce) and [LeadMagic](/reviews/leadmagic) were
more accurate still. For consumer lists where price decides,
[MillionVerifier](/reviews/millionverifier) gives almost the same answers
for less.
