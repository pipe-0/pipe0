## What is Apollo?

Apollo is a B2B contact database with a sales engagement suite built around
it. You search its 240 million contacts by title, company size, industry, or
technology, reveal emails and phone numbers, and send them into sequences,
calls, and meetings without leaving the app. Tim Zheng and Ray Li founded it
in 2015; Matt Curl took over as CEO in February 2026.

The appeal is consolidation. A founder or a three-person sales team gets a
database, a sequencer, a dialer, a meeting scheduler, and a Chrome extension
on one bill, starting at nothing.

## Where Apollo's data comes from

Apollo builds its database from four sources, according to its own data
overview: a contributor network of more than two million users who share
email and calendar signals, engagement data from its own tools (replies and
bounces), public web crawling, and third-party providers. Paid plans add a
waterfall that checks outside partners for missing data.

Our read of that mix: the data is best where Apollo's users are, and in our
experience that means US tech companies. Coverage gets thinner for European
contacts, small businesses, and uncommon job titles, where fewer users
contribute and fewer signals come back. We haven't benchmarked Apollo, so
test it on your own market.

On accuracy, Apollo contradicts itself. Its data page claims 98% email
accuracy. Its help center article on email verification, updated in
September 2026, describes a seven-step process and "a 91% accuracy rate".
Both may be true for different samples. Neither is a reason to skip a
verification pass on a list you plan to send to.

## What Apollo costs in practice

Apollo prices per seat, and every seat comes with credits. An email costs 1
credit and a mobile number 8, and credits are only charged when Apollo finds
something. On Basic at $49 a seat billed annually, a seat's 2,500 credits
come to about 2 cents per email or 16 cents per mobile. Embedded waterfalls
in other tools typically charge 8 to 30 cents per email and 25 cents to a
dollar per mobile.

Two details shape the real bill. Credits don't roll over, so a seat that
uses half its credits pays double per contact. And credits are tied to
seats, so a team that needs more data but not more people either buys
add-on credits (1.5 to 2.5 cents each) or pays for seats nobody logs into.
The Organization plan needs three seats on an annual contract.

## Apollo for agents

Apollo is one of the more agent-ready tools in the category. Its MCP server
at `mcp.apollo.io/mcp` works with Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, and Claude Code,
and lets an agent search the database, enrich contacts, add them to
sequences, and update the CRM. It is available on every plan, including
Free, which makes it the cheapest way to try agentic prospecting on real
data. The REST API is also on every plan; the free tier is limited to 600
requests a day per endpoint.

What an agent can't do is reach past Apollo's database. If Apollo doesn't
have the mobile number, the agent doesn't either.

## Apollo vs pipe0

pipe0 does not replace Apollo's sequences or dialer; it hands enriched lists
to outreach tools. Where they overlap is data. Apollo answers every lookup
from one database. pipe0 runs waterfalls across 50+ providers and stops at
the first that answers, so coverage keeps growing where a single database
runs out, and you pay only for the provider that found the result. Sheets
hold up to 2 million rows and run enrichment on schedules into HubSpot,
Salesforce, or Attio.

Many teams use both: pipe0 to find and verify the data, Apollo to send. The
[full comparison](/compare/pipe0-vs-apollo) covers the differences.

## Who should buy Apollo

Buy Apollo if you are a small team selling into US tech and want one tool
for finding people and contacting them. Start on the free plan and check its
coverage of twenty accounts you know well before you pay.

Look elsewhere if your market is Europe or small businesses, if you need
mobile numbers at high coverage, or if your enrichment needs go beyond a
contact lookup. Then a waterfall, or a table like [Clay](/reviews/clay),
will return more.
