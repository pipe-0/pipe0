## What is Tomba?

Tomba is an email finder and verifier in the mould of Hunter. Give it a name
and a company domain, a LinkedIn profile URL or a domain, and it returns the
most likely address with a score, a verification status and the web pages
it found it on. Tomba technology web service LLC was founded in 2021 and
says 150,000 professionals use it.

Around the finder sit a verifier, a phone finder, a domain search, browser
extensions and integrations, plus an API with 13 SDKs and an MCP server.

## How it did in our tests

Tomba's free plan allows 2 requests a minute and 10 a day per endpoint, so
we could not run it across our full benchmark. We have two smaller views of
its data instead.

The first is direct. Of 5 LinkedIn lookups, Tomba found 3 addresses: one
matched what other providers returned, one was a Hotmail address, and one
was a two-letter mailbox on a domain that accepts all mail. Its verifier
called all 10 real work addresses we checked valid, the same verdict
ZeroBounce gave.

The second is through [Treg](/reviews/treg), whose waterfalls call Tomba
after cheaper providers miss. There Tomba answered 17 work email lookups and
22 mobile lookups. 6 of the 17 emails were personal mailboxes, such as
`@gmail.com` and `@yahoo.com`, and only 3 were on the person's company
domain. All 22 phone numbers were valid, 18 of them mobiles, but only 9
matched a number another provider found for the same person.

These are the harder cases, people the cheaper providers could not find,
so they are not a fair measure of Tomba's overall coverage. They do show
how it fails: when it isn't sure, it still returns an address, and that
address is often a personal one.

## What it costs

Basic costs $49 a month, or $37 billed yearly, for 2,500 searches and 2,500
verifications, about 2 cents a search. Growth at $89 adds the phone finder,
and Pro at $199 covers 20,000 of each. A finder search is free when nothing
comes back, but plan credits reset every month.

## Who should use Tomba

Use Tomba if you find addresses from names and domains on a small budget and
verify everything before you send. Its pricing and API make it an easy
first step.

Look elsewhere if the address goes straight into a sequence. In our tests
too many of its work emails were personal mailboxes, and for phones,
[Surfe](/reviews/surfe) and [Wiza](/reviews/wiza) agreed with other sources
far more often.
