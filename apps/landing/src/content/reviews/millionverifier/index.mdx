## What is MillionVerifier?

MillionVerifier is an email verification service run from Hungary by Tamás
Hám-Szabó, built for one thing: checking very large lists for very little
money. Upload a file or call a single GET endpoint, and each address comes
back as ok, catch-all, unknown, disposable, invalid, or error, with a quality
flag. It says more than 70,000 businesses use it, and it connects to
newsletter tools, CRMs, and sequencers, including Clay.

There is no subscription. You buy a pack of credits, and they never expire.

## How it did in our test

We ran 100 labelled addresses through three verifiers. Fifty were real
mailboxes, pipe0 users who had signed in with Google in the last 45 days.
Fifty were fake: random mailboxes at Gmail, Outlook, and well-known company
domains, and addresses at domains that don't exist.

On the easy half, MillionVerifier was perfect. All 20 real Gmail addresses
came back valid, and all 20 fake freemail addresses and all 10 dead domains
came back invalid. For a consumer newsletter list, that is the job.

Work addresses were different. Of 30 real work addresses, MillionVerifier
confirmed 11 and called 18 catch-all. Of 20 fake ones, it caught 5 and again
called 13 catch-all. It gives that verdict when a mail server accepts the
test without confirming the mailbox, which many company domains do. The
other two verifiers resolved almost all of the same addresses.

So the overall score, 66 of 100, says less about errors than about caution.
MillionVerifier rejected only one real address, the fewest of the three. It
just declined to commit on a third of the list. In practice, that leaves you
two bad choices on a work list: send only to its valid results and lose 19
of 50 real leads, or send to catch-alls as well and let 14 fakes through.

## What it costs

This is where MillionVerifier wins outright. 10,000 checks cost $39, 100,000
cost $149, and a million cost $449. ZeroBounce charges $129, $649, and
$3,199 for the same volumes. At 25 million and up, a check costs 0.02 cents.
Unknown and catch-all results are credited back as goodwill credits, which
MillionVerifier's terms say it can withdraw.

The money-back guarantee is real but narrow: it refunds your last payment if
more than 4% of the valid addresses hard-bounce, as long as you uploaded the
file in the web app and mailed every valid address within seven days.

## MillionVerifier for developers

The real-time API is a single GET request that allows 160 requests a second
and answered in a median of 0.7 seconds in our test. A bulk API handles
files. There are code samples in the docs, but no SDKs and no official MCP
server, so an agent reaches it through a generic HTTP tool or a third-party
bridge.

One thing to check before you send it customer data: its help center says
data is never processed outside the EU, while its own published list of
processors includes US and Canadian hosts and three AI vendors.

## MillionVerifier and pipe0

We buy verification from MillionVerifier, and pipe0 offers it as a pipe. It
is the right choice for consumer addresses and list hygiene at volume. For
work addresses, this benchmark is why we point outbound teams at
[ZeroBounce](/reviews/zerobounce) instead.

## Who should buy MillionVerifier

Buy MillionVerifier if you clean large lists of mostly consumer addresses,
or you want to remove dead domains and obvious junk before a send at the
lowest possible price.

Look elsewhere if your list is work addresses for cold outbound. There, a
third of its answers will be catch-all, and you'll need a second verifier for
those anyway.
