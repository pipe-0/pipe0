## What is BuiltWith?

BuiltWith is an Australian company that crawls the web and records the
technologies each website uses: shop platforms, CRMs, analytics, payment
providers, ad pixels and hosting. It sells that data as lead lists, market
reports and APIs.

Its Domain API takes a domain and returns every technology BuiltWith has
detected on it, each with the date it was first and last seen.

## How good is BuiltWith's tech-stack data?

The most complete we tested. On 10 SaaS companies and 10 online stores it
found a technology in 83% and 76% of the prospecting categories we checked:
CRM and marketing automation, payments, email, reviews, analytics and ad
pixels. DataForSEO found one in 13 to 14%, mostly the website framework.
Amplemarket came close on coverage but has no detection dates and listed
conflicting shop platforms for three of the ten stores.

## What should you watch out for?

Old data. BuiltWith keeps every technology it has ever detected, so by
default a Shopify store still shows the Magento it left years ago. Only 15
to 45% of the entries were seen in the last 90 days. Ask for live
technologies only, with the LIVEONLY flag, before you filter on a tool.

Noise. Popularity rankings, CDN edge locations, server countries,
currencies and copyright years come back as technologies. They aren't tools
anyone chose, and they bury the ones that matter.

## How much does BuiltWith cost?

Every Domain API lookup uses one credit, and failed lookups use none.
Credits cost $0.0495 each pay as you go and less in larger packs; the Team
plan, $995 per month with 100,000 credits, works out to about $0.01 per
lookup. The list plans from $295 per month are for building lead lists in
BuiltWith's app.

## BuiltWith and pipe0

pipe0 buys from BuiltWith and offers its Domain API as the
[tech stack pipe](/docs/pipe-catalog/company:techstack:builtwith/2). It
asks BuiltWith for technologies currently in use by default and drops the
entries that aren't tools, so a company comes back as about 120
recognisable tools instead of 400 to 500 entries, grouped by type. You can
widen it to the last 12 months or the full history, and run it on every row
of a sheet or through an agent.
