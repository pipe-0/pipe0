## What is Surfe?

Surfe started as a Chrome extension that moves LinkedIn profiles into a CRM,
and that is still how most of its 50,000+ users meet it. Open a profile, and
Surfe shows the person's work email and mobile number, checks whether they
are already in HubSpot or Salesforce, and adds them with one click. Around
that it has grown a web app, people and company search, an API, and an MCP
server.

David Chevalier and Romain Ginestou run it from offices in Paris, Barcelona,
and New York. It is Pipedrive's official enrichment provider and supplies
data to HubSpot's prospecting agent, which says something about how other
software companies rate its data.

## Why its coverage is so high

Surfe is not a database. It is a waterfall. Each email lookup runs through
Findymail, LeadMagic, Hunter, RocketReach, Prospeo, Wiza, and DropContact in
that order, and every address is checked by ZeroBounce before it comes back.
Mobile numbers go through a different chain of nine providers, including
People Data Labs, ContactOut, and Datagma. A source that misses hands the
person to the next one.

That design shows in our benchmark. We ran the same 150 LinkedIn profiles
through nine providers, and Surfe found a mobile number for 144 of them. The
next best, at 79%, was a single premium source. Surfe's numbers also matched
what other providers returned 74% of the time, the highest agreement of any
provider above 50% coverage. It led the work email test too, and finding a
LinkedIn profile from an email address.

On personal email it tied for first, with an address for half the profiles
and 81% agreement with the other providers. Finding the LinkedIn profile
behind an email address is the hardest test we run, and Surfe led that one
too, at 25% with 87% agreement.

## Where it falls short

Speed is the first trade-off. Every Surfe enrichment is an asynchronous job:
you submit people, then poll or wait for a webhook. Phone numbers came back
in a median of 4.4 seconds in our test, and email lookups take longer. That
is fine for a list or a CRM sync. It is too slow for anything a user waits
on.

Pricing is the second. Credits come per user: 150 emails and 50 mobile
numbers a month on Essential, 1,000 and 100 on Pro. They reset on the
billing date, and top-ups expire after three months, at a price Surfe shows
only inside the app. A team where one person runs enrichment for everyone
ends up buying seats for credits.

## Surfe for developers and agents

The API is documented and built for volume: batches of up to 10,000 people,
10 requests a second, and up to 50,000 enrichments a day, with webhooks so
you don't have to poll. Misses are free on every path we tested. The MCP
server, still in beta, gives Claude, Cursor, and other clients the same
search and enrichment tools with the same key.

What is unclear is which plan you need. The pricing page lists API access
under Enterprise. The developer docs let any account create a key, and
Surfe's own help center says it is still aligning API quotas with its
Essential and Pro plans. Ask before you build on it.

## Surfe and pipe0

We buy data from Surfe. In pipe0's default mobile waterfall it runs second,
after Wiza: Wiza answers most profiles more cheaply, and Surfe finds the
numbers Wiza misses. It is also in our work email and role-finding
waterfalls. We put it there because of the benchmark above, and the scores
on this page come from the same numbers.

If you want Surfe's data without its per-user credits, pipe0 is one way to
get it: you pay per result found, and Surfe sits inside a waterfall you can
see and reorder.

## Who should buy Surfe

Buy Surfe if your reps prospect on LinkedIn and work in HubSpot, Salesforce,
or Pipedrive, and mobile coverage matters to your pipeline. It is the
easiest way we know to put the best contact data we have measured in front
of a salesperson.

Look elsewhere if you need answers in under a second, if a few people do
the enrichment for a large team, or if you want to choose the providers
yourself. Then an API waterfall, or [Crustdata](/reviews/crustdata) for
company data, is the better fit.
